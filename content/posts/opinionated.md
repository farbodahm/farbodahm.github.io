I keep ending up in the same conversation with friends and colleagues. So I'm writing it down as a short post that I can point to next time.

The conversation usually looks like this. Two people argue about something, both are sure they are right, and half an hour later nobody has changed their mind. Most of the time, they are arguing as if there is a right answer, when there isn't one.

I believe things around us are opinionated way more than we think. What do I mean by this?

## Two kinds of questions

When we talk about any subject, one of these two is true:

- We can prove or measure whether it's right or wrong.
- Or we are talking about it based on an opinion.

"Which route is shorter?" is the first kind. Open the map and you're done. "Is it worth taking the longer route because it has a nicer view?" is the second kind. Nobody can prove it.

Now look at how many things are in the second group:

- Hiring a new candidate. Two interviewers can sit in the same interview and come out with opposite decisions.
- What's worth spending money on
- Whether a vacation should be relaxing or packed with activities
- Career advice from people who succeeded. They tell you what worked for them. The people it didn't work for usually don't give the advice.
- Someone's tweets
- How much to use AI
- The default settings in the apps and devices you use every day. Someone picked every one of them.
- Operating systems (yes, [Omarchy](https://omarchy.org/)!)
- The abstractions behind software libraries
- Code reviews. Outside of actual bugs, arguing whether one 10-line function is "cleaner" than three 3-line helpers.

I'm only talking about topics that are debatable and usually end in an argument. Whether broccoli is delicious, or whether blue is more beautiful than red, isn't what I mean here :)

The quick test I use: **is there something we could measure or look up that would settle this?** If yes, stop arguing and go check. If no, you're talking about an opinion, and the conversation should change.

## Opinions should have a reason

Back at university, I had a great OS professor who taught me two things:

- An engineer's feet should always be on the ground.
- You can't look at a lamp and estimate!

By the second one, he meant that you can't just estimate things without any reason or metric behind your number.

I want to generalize this. Saying something is opinionated doesn't mean anything goes. If you have an opinion, it should come from good evidence, like your own experience or some statistics.

You can't say "this restaurant is bad because it's rainy today". (Yeah, you get what I mean!) You can say "*I think* this restaurant is bad, because I've been there three times and waited an hour for my food each time."

And yes, even that is opinionated. Is an hour too long? Some people don't mind waiting if the food is good. But now there is something concrete to talk about.

## Tradeoffs and reaching a consensus

If you can convince the person you're arguing with that you're both talking about an opinionated subject, you have both accepted that there isn't a true/false answer. Otherwise it wouldn't be an opinionated subject.

So the question changes from "who is right?" to "what are we trading?". Take the vacation from the list:

- A relaxing one: you come back rested. But you may feel that you didn't really see the place.
- A packed one: you see a lot and come back with stories. But you may need another vacation after it.

Neither is the correct way to travel. The useful question is which cost hurts more *for us, on this trip*. That one can be answered.

Sometimes the easiest way out is to take turns. You pick the restaurant tonight, I pick the next one. Nobody had to be right, and we still got dinner.

And sometimes you just accept the other opinion. Maybe you couldn't convince them, or you don't have the energy for it right now. That's okay. Say what you think and why, then go with the decision. Losing an opinionated argument doesn't mean you were wrong. The decision just went the other way.

## Why am I writing this?

Accepting that things are opinionated helped me be calmer. I argue over the things that really matter, and I accept what I can't change.

So next time we're in the middle of an argument and I send you this link, now you know why :))

PS: And yes, this post is also opinionated.
